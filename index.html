Product case study

Why this exists

Problem. Getting dressed well takes knowing three things at once: what actually suits your body and coloring, what you already own, and what's worth buying next. Most people never connect all three, they either shop randomly or default to the same five outfits.

Scope decision, the important one. Body shape and undertone are self-selected here, not detected from a photo, and there's no feature that generates a photo of you wearing an outfit either. That second one was a deliberate cut, not a missing feature: realistic person-plus-garment image generation is a genuinely hard, compute-heavy ML problem on its own, and shipping it as a public, unauthenticated demo means anyone can upload a photo of anyone. That's the exact shape of feature that gets misused for non-consensual imagery of real people. Self-select and swatch-based matching get almost all of the user value without either the bias risk of photo-based skin-tone detection or that misuse surface.

What I built. The matching mechanic reuses the same core loop as my other project, Use It Up: what you have vs. what a formula needs, applied to a different domain, closet items instead of pantry items, outfit formulas instead of recipes, a shopping list of missing pieces instead of a grocery list. Recognizing that pattern generalizes across two unrelated product ideas was the actual insight, not the styling content itself.

What I'd add next. A real e-commerce API so shopping links point at actual in-stock items instead of a search page, and if photo-based features ever get revisited, a proper bias and misuse review before shipping, not after.
